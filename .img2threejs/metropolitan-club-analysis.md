# Metropolitan Club reference analysis

## Identification

The target is the Metropolitan Club clubhouse at Fifth Avenue and East 60th
Street: a static, hard-surface architectural object. Confidence is high for
the visible Fifth Avenue and 60th Street elevations. The requested output is
an approximate, low-poly real-time reconstruction, not measured conservation
documentation.

## Overall form

The visible main block is a near-rectangular four-storey marble palazzo. Its
street elevations are bilateral grids: five bays on Fifth Avenue and seven on
60th Street. The reference has a tall rusticated base, two very tall principal
storeys, a short attic window band, and a deeply projecting cornice capped in
oxidized copper.

## Component hierarchy

- Macro: rectangular marble main block; rusticated base; upper marble wall;
  attic; projecting cornice/copper cap.
- Meso: five- and seven-bay window grids; belt courses; corner quoins; central
  balcony; hooded principal windows; entrance portico on 60th Street.
- Micro: balcony balusters and brackets; cornice dentils/modillions; attic
  rosettes/paterae; window pediments and keystones; oxidized copper crest edge.

## Spatial relationships

The rusticated base supports and is flush with the upper wall. Belt courses
overlap the wall slightly. Window recesses sit just proud of the wall as an
opaque game abstraction. Quoins overlap both street corners. Balcony brackets
embed in the wall and support the balcony slab; balusters socket into the
balcony base and rail. The cornice overlaps the wall and projects on every
visible face; the copper cap sits on and overlaps the cornice crown.

## Materials and finish

- Vermont/Tuckahoe marble: warm off-white, low saturation, dielectric,
  roughness approximately 0.78-0.9; broad blocks rather than fine noise.
- Window sash/recess: painted dark wood and dark blue-gray opaque composite,
  matching the project's performant window convention.
- Copper roof/cornice cap: oxidized blue-green patina, metallic response muted
  by roughness and weathering.
- Iron balcony/flag hardware: very dark gray painted metal.

## Identity features

1. Monumental four-storey proportions rather than the current two-storey box.
2. Five-by-seven bay hierarchy.
3. Rusticated marble base and strong first belt course.
4. Tall hooded principal-storey windows.
5. Central balcony with brackets and balustrade.
6. Small square attic window band.
7. Corner quoins.
8. Six-foot-equivalent deep classical cornice.
9. Oxidized copper roofline/cap.

## Uncertainty

The single modern photograph does not show the original rear elevation,
courtyard, roof plan, or the full 60th Street entrance. Modern adjacent towers
also obscure the historical skyline. Hidden faces will therefore be restrained
continuations of the visible grammar. The reconstruction will not claim exact
ornament, stone joint measurements, or the later annex.
