# Reagent bottle rack image analysis

Status: provisional ImageGen concept, not historical evidence.

## Observation

- One freestanding wooden rack, seen from a front three-quarter view.
- The rack is wider than it is tall and has a long base, a rear board, two side cheeks, a low front rail, and repeated vertical dividers.
- Six separate narrow-necked glass bottles sit in a single row. Their bodies are cylindrical to gently shouldered, with rounded transitions and thick rolled lips.
- Each bottle has a cork stopper, an aged paper label curved around its body, and a distinct liquid level and colour.
- The retaining rail crosses below the label centres, so the bottle labels remain readable.
- Wood members visibly overlap at their contacts. Rounded edges and a dark contact shadow keep the rack from reading as flat panels.

## Material reading

- Wood: warm unfinished deal, high roughness, visible grain and scratches, darker recesses, lighter worn edges.
- Glass: colourless to faint warm-grey dielectric, low roughness, strong environment highlights, visible transmission and thickness cues.
- Liquids: transparent amber, ochre, brown, pale green, and nearly clear volumes with darker depth and a meniscus.
- Cork: warm brown, matte and porous.
- Labels: warm off-white paper, matte, slightly mottled, dark ruled border; the concept labels are intentionally blank.

## Identity features

- Wide rack silhouette with raised side cheeks and rear support.
- Repeated bottle bays and separate removable bottle forms.
- Front retaining rail placed below the paper labels.
- Varied bottle shoulder profiles, heights, liquid levels, and liquid hues.
- Rolled glass lips, cork stoppers, curved labels, and visible menisci.
- Independent wood, glass, liquid, cork, and paper material responses.

## Inference and limits

- The rear joinery, underside, exact dimensions, and any historic manufacturer details are hidden.
- The ornamental rack plaques are concept decoration, not supported evidence, and will be omitted.
- A second row and empty slots are useful procedural extensions, not observations from this image.
- The implementation should preserve editable named bottle slots so bottles can later become individually interactive without remodelling the rack.
